ADAPTATION 1, RE-EXAMINED FOR THE PASSIVE LEG. Prompt 65 translated the xg suite's mean-multiple threshold convention onto the xT target via xg's own standard-deviation fraction, because the xT target's mean is near zero and a mean-multiple threshold would be an arbitrary sliver. That justification holds on this leg in its ORIGINAL form: target_xt_delta_passive's mean is -0.002674, NEGATIVE as v1's was and unlike active v2's positive mean, so '0.5 x the overall mean' would flip the sign of every threshold. At -0.002674 against a std of 0.036748 the mean is only 7.3% of one standard deviation. Mechanically: xg's own flat_margin (0.5 x mean xg = 0.002996 on these same rows) re-expressed as a fraction of xg's own std (0.040231) gives 0.074461, applied to target_xt_delta_passive's std (0.036748) to give 0.002736. Every number is computed at runtime from the real parquet -- nothing is copied from the active leg. See `threshold_transfer_warning` in this same block. Scale-free thresholds (|Spearman rho| >= 0.05, small-n row counts, the substitutive/additive ratio cutoffs) are carried over completely unchanged and are unaffected.

ADAPTATION 2, unchanged in method and re-measured on the passive leg. The xg_target suite's second panel conditions on target_future_shot_10s == 1 to strip the structural zero mass. target_xt_delta_passive has no shot column standing behind it; its structural-zero analogue is the 26.4% of rows with an EXACTLY zero delta -- the next same-possession event landed in the same 8x12 xT grid cell as the snapshot itself, which on this leg is very common because most on-ball events are short passes and carries. That share is LARGER than active v2's 20.2%, so the panel matters more here, not less. The conditional panel throughout this half of the portal is 'given a non-zero delta' (target_xt_delta_passive != 0), never 'given a shot'.

ADAPTATION 3, unchanged in method and re-measured on the passive leg. Sign carries the football meaning: POSITIVE target_xt_delta_passive = xT fell across the event (threat reduced, good for the defence), NEGATIVE = xT rose. On this leg the two directions are almost exactly balanced -- 36.4% positive vs 37.2% negative -- but the target is strongly LEFT-skewed (skew -2.6036), far more so than active v2's -0.306, so the negative tail is much longer than the positive one even though the counts match. Two-directional framing therefore matters more here than anywhere else in this portal. Rankings are by |magnitude| across BOTH directions, and every table also reports the negative/positive/zero share behind its mean.

NOT an adaptation, re-checked rather than inherited: the xg_target report suite never log-transforms its target anywhere, on either leg, and neither did the active xT suite. The log1p framing in this project belongs to the modelling legs, not to this report suite. This leg's excess kurtosis of ~31 makes a transform more tempting than anywhere else in the portal -- and it is still NOT applied here, because doing so would silently break comparability with the passive xg portal and with this portal's own active half. Recorded as an open question for the modelling legs, not resolved by an EDA suite.

row grain -- read every n with this in hand

one value per event, repeated across that event's defender slots — the target is computed once per event_id and left-joined onto every visible defender-slot row, exactly as target_future_shot_10s and target_future_xg_10s already are on this leg. Category n's are row counts, not independent observations.

threshold caveat -- recomputed for this leg, not inherited

the std-derived flat margin spans 62.1% of rows — 1.51x the MAD on this leg, against 1.28x and 53.4% on the active leg's own v2 target and 0.53x / 35.4% on v1. Shape labels anywhere in this half of the portal are indicative, not decided.

The active portal's Clearance finding does NOT transfer to this column, and is not forced to.

Prompt 66/67's headline active-leg result is that Clearance flips from 8th of 8 event_type categories to 1st of 8 under the corrected xt_after. A reader arriving from the active Category Atlas might reasonably look for the same row here. It is not below, and it could not be. event_type does not exist in passive_defense.parquet -- checked directly against the live schema, not assumed. This leg's nearest relative, on_ball_event_type, describes the ATTACKING action the defensive snapshot is anchored to (Pass, Carry, Ball Receipt*, Shot, ...), not a defensive action taken by the row's own defender. A Clearance row in the active dataset and a Pass row here are not the same kind of thing, and comparing their mean deltas would read like a finding while meaning nothing.

What this card does carry, on its own terms: Dribble is the most threat-REDUCING on-ball event type at +0.001548 (n=21,429, 28.7% positive), and Shot the most threat-INCREASING at -0.066888 (n=24,487, 53.6% negative). Read both as descriptions of what happens around a defensive snapshot, not as verdicts on defending: the row's target is a property of the EVENT, shared identically across every defender slot at that event.
