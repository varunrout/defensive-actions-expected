backlog vs V1

V1's needs_human_call backlog was 116 pairs, on the 51/44-feature scope (REVIEW_METHODOLOGY.html). This page's backlog is 39 pairs, on the smaller 36/39-feature scope -- the two numbers aren't purely a methodology comparison, since most of the drop is fewer features/pairs existing at all by stage 07, not Types 5/6 resolving more. The remaining 39 are genuinely open questions (mostly the passive-side top_option_2/3_threat_score family), not gaps in the resolution logic.

Correlation says two features move together -- never which one (or whether either) matters. Every verdict on this page traces to a lift number (the actual shot-rate difference), not the r-value alone. The r-value only got a pair onto this list; it never decided the verdict.

The five relationship types

Types 1, 2 and 4 are from V1; Types 5 and 6 (V2) close the two gaps V1 left open -- categorical pairs that were tiered and then never processed, and a blanket "no rule" fallback for continuous↔continuous pairs.

Type 1

Boolean vs. its continuous parent

Boolean↔continuous pair, |r| ≥ 0.75, and the continuous side isn't itself scheduled for removal elsewhere. Structural call, no lift lookup needed.

Verdict rule: Drop the boolean, keep the continuous.

No Type 1 example resolved in this run.
Type 2

Boolean vs. boolean, same rough concept

Both boolean, r ≥ 0.5. Checked in order: opposite-sign lift → keep both. Same sign with one flag a near-total subset of the other (containment ≥ 90%) → keep the higher-signal flag. Same sign, lift diff < 1.0pp → near-identical, drop the broader flag. Same sign, diff ≥ 3.0pp, no subset → keep both. Diff between 1.0pp and 3.0pp → needs_human_call.

Verdict rule: Depends on lift comparison -- see evidence ledger below.

No Type 2 drop example in this run.
Type 4

State-persistence pairs

Same base column paired with its own _prev_event / lagged version, detected by name pattern.

Verdict rule: Keep both, always -- this is autocorrelation, not redundancy.

phase_label vs phase_label_prev_event (cramers_v) -- kept both, the persistence relationship this rule protects.
Type 5 (V2)

Categorical vs. its correlated continuous/boolean context

Conceptual, not statistical: does the categorical column carry information beyond what's already in the continuous/boolean measurement, or is it just a relabeling of the same thing? Now also reaches into COLLAPSE (categorical pairs used to be tiered and then never processed by anything). Default keep both; flagged needs_human_call when η ≥ 0.85 or Cramér's V ≥ 0.7.

Verdict rule: Keep both by default; needs_human_call above the relabeling threshold.

event_type vs nearest_attacker_distance: correlation_ratio=0.5443 → keep both.
Type 6 (V2)

Continuous vs. continuous, same engineered family

Both continuous/discrete, and both columns appear together in an explicit, hardcoded family group (feature_config.FAMILY_GROUPS) -- e.g. *_within_5m vs *_within_10m, or a ranked-option column at different ranks. Default keep both (deferred to feature-importance evidence); needs_human_call if |r| ≥ 0.90, or if no family group matches at all.

Verdict rule: Keep both if same family and |r| < 0.90; needs_human_call otherwise -- never forces a family match that isn't real.

defenders_within_5m vs defenders_within_10m: r=0.6046 → keep both (same engineered family).

Structural Redesign

Some REVIEW-tier (and adjacent) pairs aren't redundancy problems at all -- the fix is "measure this differently," not "drop one side." Two root causes showed up in the passive-side top_option_1/2/3_* family: absolute pitch coordinates encoded near a moving reference point (the ball), and several ranked/repeated columns describing the same real-world cluster of objects (ranked attacking options).

Part A -- DONE

top_option_{1,2,3}_target_x/y were absolute pitch coordinates, which is why they correlated 0.68-0.88 with ball_x and with each other -- the same absolute position meant something different depending on where the ball was. Replaced with top_option_n_dx/dy (offset from the ball), top_option_n_distance_from_ball, and top_option_n_angle_from_ball, kept alongside the raw columns during a transition period, then the raw target_x/y columns were dropped from the candidate feature list once the fix was confirmed working -- correlation with ball_x/defender_x is gone from the output entirely, not just re-tiered.

Part B -- still deferred, Cluster 5 (3 pairs)

The top_option_1/2/3_threat_score inter-rank correlations were never a coordinate-encoding problem -- they're a genuine question of whether ranks 2-3 carry independent signal or are redundant with rank 1. Still open:

  • top_option_2_threat_score ↔ top_option_3_threat_score (r=0.8874)
  • top_option_1_threat_score ↔ top_option_2_threat_score (r=0.8747)
  • top_option_1_threat_score ↔ top_option_3_threat_score (r=0.8049)
Done

Ball-relative coordinate transform (Part A) -- unambiguous, lost no information, confirmed working by re-running the correlation analysis after the drop.

Still deferred

Collapsing top_option_2/3_* into aggregate "backup option" features (max threat score, spread vs rank 1) is a real tradeoff -- it may throw away signal a model would otherwise use. Gated behind PASSIVE_COLLAPSE_OPTION_RANKS (default False) until a baseline model's feature importance says whether ranks 2-3 are pulling their weight. Type 6's review logic deliberately keeps these pairs in needs_human_call regardless of |r| -- correlation alone was never going to be what decides this.

Recommendation: no action until a baseline model exists. Do not collapse ranks 2-3 from correlation alone; correlation cannot tell you whether they're pulling weight, only that they move together.
